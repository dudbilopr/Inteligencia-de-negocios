Short Reading: The Power of Preattentive Attributes

In data visualization, preattentive attributes are visual properties that our brains process in milliseconds, before we even realize we are paying attention. According to Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic (Storytelling with Data), these attributes—such as color, size, shape, and spatial position—can be leveraged to direct the audience's attention exactly where you want it.

However, preattentive attributes lose their power if overused. If everything is highlighted, nothing is highlighted. This ties into the concept of clutter (cognitive load). Clutter refers to visual elements that take up space but don't increase understanding. The Data-Ink Ratio principle suggests that you should maximize the ink used to present data and minimize the ink used for non-data elements (like heavy gridlines or 3D effects).
